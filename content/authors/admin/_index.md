Hello, I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Engineering at [Colorado State University](https://www.engr.colostate.edu/), advised by Professor [Anthony A. Maciejewski](https://www.engr.colostate.edu/ece/people/tony-maciejewski/) and Professor [Agnieszka Miguel](https://www.seattleu.edu/directory/profiles/agnieszka-miguel-phd.php) at [Seattle University](https://www.seattleu.edu/science-engineering/academic-departments/department-of-electrical-and-computer-engineering/). My research focuses on developing efficient algorithms for individual animal identification, with a particular emphasis on small-scale, unlabeled camera-trap datasets of African leopards. I aim to reduce human annotation effort while preserving high accuracy by leveraging clustering, verification, and human-in-the-loop supervision. In addition, I actively contribute to the academic community through paper reviewing and service as an associate editor for the course [International Conference on Systems, Man, and Cybernetics (SMC) ](https://www.ieeesmc.org/conferences/). I have also gained teaching experience as a teaching assistant for the course [Introduction to Robot Programming/Simulation, ECE 455](https://online.colostate.edu/courses/ECE/ECE455.dot). Previously, I received my B.S. degree in Electrical Engineering and Automation from [University of Jinan](https://cse.ujn.edu.cn/index.htm). 

Beyond research, I enjoyed traveling, hiking, and photography, often documenting the world through my own experiences. Hiking, in particular, resonates with me: even when I know where the summit lies, the climb can still be a struggle, and on the way back, although the hardest part is behind me, my energy is nearly spent. To me, this mirrors the research journey, challenging, exhausting, but ultimately rewarding.
